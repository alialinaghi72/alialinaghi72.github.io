Mineral resources vs ore reserves under the JORC Code

In everyday speech a "reserve" is any quantity of mineral, but the international reporting codes give the word a precise meaning. Confusing resources with reserves is one of the most common reasons mining projects are misjudged.

Key takeaways

  • A Mineral Resource has reasonable prospects for eventual economic extraction; an Ore Reserve is the part of it shown to be economically mineable by a techno-economic study.
  • Resources are classified by geological confidence as Inferred, Indicated and Measured.
  • Only Indicated and Measured Resources, after applying the Modifying Factors, convert to Probable and Proved Reserves.
  • Public reports under JORC must be based on the work of, and taken responsibility for by, a Competent Person.
  • The three-digit codes common in Iranian reports (111, 122, 333 and so on) come from the UNFC framework and have approximate JORC equivalents.

Why classification matters

Investors, banks and buyers need numbers whose meaning is clear and comparable. The CRIRSCO family of reporting codes — JORC in Australasia, NI 43-101 with the CIM definitions in Canada, SAMREC in South Africa, PERC in Europe and S-K 1300 in the United States — share common definitions so that a "Measured Resource" means broadly the same thing everywhere.

Exploration results, mineral resources and ore reserves

  • Exploration Results: data and information from exploration — drillhole and sample results — not yet estimated as a Mineral Resource.
  • Mineral Resource: a concentration or occurrence of material of economic interest whose form, quality and quantity give reasonable prospects for eventual economic extraction.
  • Ore Reserve: the economically mineable part of a Measured or Indicated Resource, including dilution and mining losses, demonstrated by at least a Pre-Feasibility Study. NI 43-101 calls it a Mineral Reserve.

Mineral resource categories

  • Inferred: quantity and grade estimated from limited geological evidence and sampling; geological and grade continuity are implied but not verified. Inferred Resources do not convert to reserves, although most are expected to be upgraded with further exploration.
  • Indicated: confidence sufficient to apply the Modifying Factors in enough detail for mine planning and economic evaluation.
  • Measured: geological and grade continuity confirmed with high confidence, sufficient for detailed mine planning and final evaluation.

The codes set no fixed drill spacing for each category. Classification combines geological judgement with quantitative criteria: drill spacing relative to the variogram range, the number of holes and samples used, kriging variance and slope of regression, the quality of QA/QC data and the adequacy of density data.

Modifying factors and reserve categories

The Modifying Factors are the considerations that convert a resource into a reserve: mining, processing, metallurgical, infrastructure, economic, marketing, legal, environmental, social and governmental factors.

  • Probable Ore Reserve: the economic part of an Indicated Resource and, in some circumstances, of a Measured Resource whose Modifying Factors carry uncertainty.
  • Proved Ore Reserve: the economic part of a Measured Resource, with the highest level of confidence.

The Competent Person

Under the JORC Code, public reports of Exploration Results, Mineral Resources and Ore Reserves must be based on the work of a Competent Person: a member of the AusIMM, the AIG or a Recognised Professional Organisation with at least five years of experience relevant to the style of mineralisation, type of deposit and activity for which they take responsibility. NI 43-101's equivalent is the Qualified Person.

UNFC equivalents used in Iranian reports

Many Iranian exploration reports state reserves with three-digit codes from the United Nations Framework Classification (UNFC, 1997 edition). The first digit is the economic axis (E), the second the feasibility axis (F) and the third the geological confidence axis (G). Approximate JORC equivalents:

The match is not one-to-one; codes 211 and 221, for example, have no exact JORC home. Converting a national report to an international standard requires a Competent Person to review the data, QA/QC and economic assumptions.

Common reporting mistakes

  • Calling something a "reserve" without a techno-economic study; a purely geological estimate is a resource, not a reserve.
  • Adding Inferred Resources to higher categories, or using them in a reserve.
  • Reporting resources without a cut-off or a realistic mining envelope.
  • Over-optimistic classification despite weak QA/QC, density data or geological model.
  • False precision, such as tonnage to the nearest tonne; reported figures should reflect estimation uncertainty.

Questions

Can Inferred Resources be used in economic studies?

Under JORC they may be used in Scoping Studies with an explicit cautionary statement, but Inferred Resources never convert to Ore Reserves and must not support a reserve declared in a Pre-Feasibility or Feasibility Study.

Is a "proved reserve" in an Iranian report the same as a JORC Proved Reserve?

It is usually treated as the equivalent, but the criteria behind national reports and CRIRSCO codes are not identical; before comparing, check the estimation method, QA/QC and techno-economic studies behind the number.
