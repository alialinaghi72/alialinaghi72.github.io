Porphyry copper: three regions, several pulses

Aghazadeh et al. (2015) group Iran’s porphyry copper deposits into three regions: Arasbaran in the northwest, the middle segment of the Urumieh–Dokhtar magmatic arc, and Kerman in the southeast. The deposits formed in pulses tied to the closure of the Neo-Tethys ocean: Arasbaran had a late-Oligocene pulse (29–27 Ma) and an early-Miocene pulse (22–20 Ma), and the middle arc a middle-Miocene pulse (17–15 Ma)[1]. The Kerman belt’s most important period is Early–Middle Miocene[2].

Most of the ages in hand fit that framework: Sungun at 22.9–21.7 Ma (Re–Os), Kahang at about 15–14.5 Ma, and Sarcheshmeh and Miduk at 12–13.6 Ma. But Masjed Daghi has an Eocene zircon age (54.1 Ma) that conflicts with the Miocene age others propose, and the minimum age of about 21 Ma for Dalli is older than the middle-arc pulse.

Iron: three geological families

The four iron deposits in this atlas fall into three geological families. Chadormalu and Choghart are Kiruna-type iron oxide–apatite deposits of the Bafq district, formed in the Early Cambrian Kashmar–Kerman volcano-plutonic arc[3][4]. Sangan is a district of Eocene iron skarns in the Khaf–Kashmar–Bardaskan belt[5]. Gol-e-Gohar lies in the Sanandaj–Sirjan Zone, and its origin — magmatic-hydrothermal or a metamorphosed banded iron formation — is the sharpest genetic dispute in the atlas[6][7].

Different sources call one or another of these deposits “the largest”; without a common basis of comparison such labels mean little, and this atlas does not use them.

Lead–zinc: three deposits, three unresolved models

None of the three lead–zinc deposits in this atlas has a settled genetic type or a radiometric ore age[8][9][10]. Mehdiabad, in the Lower Cretaceous of the Yazd Basin, is debated between SEDEX and carbonate-replacement or Irish-type models; Irankuh, in the Aptian south of Isfahan, between MVT, syn-sedimentary and Irish-type; and Angouran, with a Neoproterozoic–Cambrian marble host and Miocene timing, between hypogene, salt-related breccia-pipe and supergene origins[11]. Angouran should therefore not share a “Cretaceous carbonate-hosted” label with the other two.

Gold: the Takab district, Sari Gunay and Muteh

Zarshuran and Agh-Darreh, 12.5 km apart in Takab County, are Iran’s clearest pair of Carlin-style gold deposits, and with the Angouran zinc deposit they form the Takab mining district[12][13]. Sari Gunay is an epithermal gold deposit in a Miocene volcanic complex (11.7–10.7 Ma)[14], and Muteh is metamorphic-hosted gold of Eocene age (55.7–38.5 Ma) in the Sanandaj–Sirjan Zone[15].

How to read this atlas

  • Figures “as published”: none of the figures in the sources reviewed states a JORC or NI 43-101 basis. Most Iranian figures use the national “proven and probable” («قطعی و احتمالی») categories, which are not equivalent to JORC classes; see resources vs reserves.
  • Contained metal: to compare conflicting figures, tonnage is multiplied by grade. This is the author’s arithmetic, and it shows which figures describe the same orebody at a different cut-off grade or scope.
  • Confidence: “high” means the figure appears in an authoritative record (a USGS database entry, Mindat or a publisher abstract) or two independent sources agree; “medium” means one reliable source or a secondary citation; “low” means news, a company website, an unclear attribution or a known conflict.
  • Coordinates: approximate, each with its source. Mehdiabad and Muteh, whose locations are inconsistent between sources, are not mapped until verified.
  • Updates: 2024–26 operating status could not be confirmed for most deposits. If you spot an error or hold newer data, please say so through the contact form or the feedback button on each page.
