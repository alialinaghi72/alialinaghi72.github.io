Mehdiabad zinc–lead deposit: geology and resources

Mehdiabad, in the southern Yazd Basin, is a deposit Maghfouri et al. describe as “world-class”. Its resource figures have changed repeatedly, even within the USGS Minerals Yearbook, and this page sets them out in date order.

Geology and mineralisation

Mehdiabad lies in the Lower Cretaceous Taft and Abkuh formations of the southern Yazd Basin[9][6]. The sulphide ore is sphalerite, galena and barite with minor pyrite and chalcopyrite; smithsonite, hydrozincite, hemimorphite and cerussite occur in the oxidised zones. Mineralisation extends over two horizons along at least 3.4 km of strike[7][3]. There are two main orebodies: the Valley Orebody of mixed sulphide and non-sulphide ore and the Mountain Orebody, which is entirely non-sulphide[5].

Deposit type: three views

Maghfouri et al. (2019) classify Mehdiabad as a “vent-proximal sub-seafloor replacement” SEDEX deposit[6]. Another study reads it as Early Cretaceous replacement of barite and dolomitised breccia[7], and Rajabi et al. place it among Irish-type deposits[8]. Reichert and Borg (2008) also discuss Mehdiabad in a study that covers Irankuh[12].

Operations and expansion

Mehdiabad’s licence sits with IMIDRO, which handed operation to a private consortium in 1395 SH (2016/17)[10]. USGS describes five private companies developing a US$1 billion expansion to 800,000 t/yr of zinc concentrate and 80,000 t/yr of lead–silver concentrate, a project delayed repeatedly since the 1990s[2]. The latest USGS Iran chapter (2023 edition, released February 2026) lists the “Mehdiabad Barite, Lead, and Zinc Complex” among IMIDRO subsidiaries[11]. The mining method was not confirmed in the sources reviewed.

Shifting USGS figures: tonnage or grade?

Expert note — the author’s analysis, not published data

Across three successive USGS yearbooks, Mehdiabad moved from about 75 Mt at 6% Zn to 160 Mt at 6% and then to 170 Mt at 4.5% Zn. Contained zinc first rose from about 4.5 to 9.6 Mt, then fell back to about 7.7 Mt as the grade was cut by a quarter. Rising tonnage with falling grade usually signals a lower cut-off grade or the addition of lower-grade material.

The literature figure (394 Mt at 4.2% Zn) is a total resource, and the 2000s figure (218 Mt at 7.2% Zn) goes back to a 1994 study; neither shares the scope of the USGS numbers. Sulphide and non-sulphide ores recover differently, so contained zinc without a split by ore type is an incomplete basis for comparison.

Exploration and estimation notes for lead–zinc deposits

Expert note — the author’s analysis, not published data

  • Sulphide versus non-sulphide ore: recovery and processing route differ, so domaining by ore type is essential.
  • Irregular geometry: strong stratigraphic and structural controls demand a detailed geological model and usually denser drilling than porphyry deposits.
  • Lead, zinc and silver separately: the Pb:Zn ratio changes in space and each metal should be estimated on its own.
  • Variable density: from heavy high-grade sulphide to porous non-sulphide ore; density must be measured for each ore type.
  • Associated elements: iron, manganese and barium can dilute ore or complicate processing and are worth logging and estimating.
