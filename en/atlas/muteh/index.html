Muteh gold deposits: geology and age

Muteh, in Isfahan Province, is a district of metamorphic-hosted gold deposits that the 1977 Minerals Yearbook described as a gold–pyrite–quartz vein deposit. Moritz et al. (2006) dated the mineralisation to the Eocene, yet even the exact location of its mines is inconsistent between databases.

Geology and age

The Muteh gold deposits sit in schist and gneiss with amphibolite and quartzite, intruded by leucogranite[9]. Using muscovite Ar–Ar ages of 55.7–38.5 Ma, Moritz et al. (2006) dated the mineralisation to the Eocene, found gold to be the youngest dated event and tied it to late extension and exhumation; their results refute earlier Precambrian–Paleozoic models[6]. Other authors call the district orogenic[7][8].

Alteration and mineralisation

Alteration comprises quartz, muscovite, pyrite, dolomite–ankerite and albite, and pyrite is the main gold host[6]. Early Senjedeh pyrite carries up to about 810 ppm Au (low–medium confidence)[12].

What an Eocene age means for exploration

Expert note — the author’s analysis, not published data

If mineralisation is tied to Eocene extension and exhumation, as Moritz et al. showed, its main controls are shear zones and extensional faults of that age, not the older metamorphic layering. That view points exploration towards Eocene structures along the Sanandaj–Sirjan Zone. For estimation, strong structural control means strong anisotropy and short continuity across the veins; with annual output of a few hundred kilograms, the accuracy of the vein model and dilution control decide the mine’s economics.

Exploration and estimation notes for metamorphic-hosted vein gold

Expert note — the author’s analysis, not published data

  • High nugget: free, coarse gold calls for large enough samples and suitable assay methods such as screen fire assay.
  • Shear-zone control: the strike and dip of veins and structures create strong anisotropy that must be reflected in variography and the search ellipse.
  • Estimation method: indicator kriging or simulation with careful top-cutting usually gives a more realistic picture than ordinary kriging.
