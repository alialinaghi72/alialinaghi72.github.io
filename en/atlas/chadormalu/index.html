Chadormalu magnetite–apatite deposit: geology and age

Chadormalu, about 180 km northeast of Yazd, is a Kiruna-type iron oxide–apatite deposit in the Bafq mining district and has the firmest ages of any iron deposit in this atlas.

Geological setting

Chadormalu and Choghart are Kiruna-type iron oxide–apatite deposits of the Bafq district, which lies within the Early Cambrian Kashmar–Kerman volcano-plutonic arc[4][5]. The host is a late Precambrian to Lower Cambrian volcano-sedimentary sequence, and sodic, calcic and potassic alteration are reported[5].

Geochronology

Chadormalu’s ages are the firmest of any iron deposit in this atlas. Apatite U–Pb gives a dominant age of 490 ± 21 Ma with disturbance ages of 437 ± 12 and 246 ± 17 Ma[3]. A 2021 study adds apatite ages of 529 ± 25 and 437 ± 21 Ma[6]. At district scale, a monazite age of 515 ± 21 Ma[4] and apatite ages of 539–527 Ma[7] have been published.

Mineralogy and alteration

The ore consists of magnetite and fluorapatite[3]. Late pyrite is enriched in sulphur-34, which the authors attribute to basinal brines[8].

Apatite: by-product or penalty?

Expert note — the author’s analysis, not published data

In iron oxide–apatite deposits phosphorus has two faces: it is a penalty element in iron concentrate and raises processing cost, but recovered apatite can be a by-product. Either way, phosphorus should be estimated alongside iron in the block model and its behaviour measured in metallurgical testwork.

The pre-mining reserve of 400 Mt at 55% Fe is a historical figure; after years of production, the remaining reserve can only be stated from an up-to-date estimate reconciled against actual production.

Exploration and estimation notes for iron deposits

Expert note — the author’s analysis, not published data

  • Density model: density changes with iron grade and its error goes straight into tonnage, so the density model matters as much as the grade model.
  • Deleterious elements: phosphorus (apatite) and sulphur control concentrate quality and processing route and should be estimated alongside iron.
  • Magnetic versus total iron: estimating saleable product needs magnetic iron and magnetic separation test results, not total iron alone.
  • Hard boundaries: ore–host contacts are often sharp, and hard-boundary modelling stops grade spreading into waste.
  • Magnetics: the main tool for exploring and delineating magnetite bodies.
