How to use the lab

  1. Pick a dataset: high-nugget vein gold, well-structured porphyry copper, or highly continuous iron.
  2. Choose a model type and move the sliders for nugget, partial sill and range.
  3. Watch the fit error; it is weighted by the number of pairs in each lag, because well-populated lags are more reliable.
  4. Compare your result with the auto-fit button.

The sample datasets

The data are synthetic but mimic three deposit types: in vein gold much of the variance sits in the nugget and the range is short; in porphyry copper the nugget is small and the range long; in iron deposits grade continuity holds over far greater distances.

Fitting in practice

On a real project, automatic fitting is only a starting point. Take the nugget from a downhole variogram, keep directions consistent with the geology, and expect to need two or three nested structures. For more detail, read the variogram explained.

Questions

How is the fit error calculated?

As the root of the mean squared difference between the experimental variogram and the model at each lag, weighted by the number of pairs in that lag.

Which model is best?

The one that fits well and is consistent with the geology. The spherical model is the most common choice for grade; the Gaussian model is rarely appropriate for grade.
