What this calculator computes

The calculator returns two numbers. The break-even cut-off includes every cost per tonne of ore — mining, processing and G&A. The marginal cut-off excludes mining cost and applies to material that has to be mined anyway, such as in-pit material removed to reach ore.

Cut-off = Costs ($/t ore) / [ (Price − Selling cost) × Recovery × Payable ]

For the full explanation and worked examples, read what is cut-off grade.

Input guide

  • Price: use a conservative long-term price for project evaluation, not the spot price. You can enter it per tonne, per pound or per troy ounce.
  • Selling cost: treatment and refining charges (TC/RC), freight and insurance, in the same unit as the price.
  • Recovery: the share of metal in the feed that reaches the product; ideally from metallurgical test work on the deposit itself.
  • Payable: the share of contained metal in the product that the buyer pays for.
  • Costs: mining, processing and G&A in dollars per tonne of ore.

The default values for each metal are illustrative only and are no substitute for your project numbers.

Limitations

This is a simplified calculation: it excludes dilution and mining recovery, mine and plant capacity constraints, capital costs and the time value of money. Polymetallic deposits need net smelter return (NSR) or metal equivalents with each metal's recovery. For investment decisions, cut-off grade should be set within full mine planning and economic analysis.

Questions

Why is the marginal cut-off lower than the break-even cut-off?

Because it excludes mining cost. Material that must be mined anyway is worth sending to the mill instead of the dump if its grade is above the marginal cut-off.

Which price unit should I use?

Whichever you have: dollars per tonne or per pound for base metals, dollars per troy ounce for gold and silver. The calculator converts units itself; just enter the selling cost in the same unit as the price.
