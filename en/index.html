Approach

How a resource estimate is built

Every stage is documented so the final figure can be traced and audited. Scroll down and the model builds itself, step by step.

Variogram
Swath plot
Low gradeHigh grade
MeasuredIndicatedInferred
  1. 01

    Data validation

    Collar, survey, lithology and assay tables are reviewed and laboratory QA/QC is checked. No data enters the model before this step.

  2. 02

    3D geological model

    Mineralised zones are modelled in 3D from lithology, alteration and structure to define the estimation domains.

  3. 03

    Statistics and variography

    Data are composited and top-cut, and the spatial continuity of grade is analysed in several directions with variograms.

  4. 04

    Block model estimation

    Each block grade is estimated by kriging or simulation, with block size and search neighbourhood tested beforehand.

  5. 05

    Validation

    The model is checked against the data: global statistics, swath plots and visual section review.

  6. 06

    Classification and reporting

    Resources are classified as Measured, Indicated and Inferred from geological confidence and data spacing, and reported.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How does a resource estimation project run?

The drillhole database and QA/QC results are validated first, then a 3D geological model is built and grade continuity analysed by variography. The block model is estimated by kriging or simulation, validated, classified against international standards and reported.

Mineral resource or ore reserve — what is the difference?

A mineral resource is the estimate of material with reasonable prospects for eventual economic extraction; a reserve is the part of the measured and indicated resource shown to be mineable once modifying factors — mine design, recovery, cut-off grade and economics — are applied.

What data is needed to start?

Collar, survey, lithology and assay tables; geological and topographic maps of the licence area; geochemical and geophysical results where available; and the laboratory QA/QC report.

Which commodities and regions?

Gold, copper, molybdenum, lead–zinc, iron, coal and talc; across Iran and neighbouring countries, with project experience in Armenia, Venezuela and Morocco.
